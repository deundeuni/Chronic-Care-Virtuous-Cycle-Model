## Chapter 1: Overview and Philosophical Background

This white paper proposes an integrated virtuous-cycle model for securing behavioral continuity and biomarker stability in the long-term management of patients with chronic disease. Conventional chronic disease management systems rely excessively on temporary treatment interventions or short-term fluctuations in indicators, and as a result showed the limitation that patients discontinue or drop out of management because of accumulated economic and psychological burdens of long-term care.

The chronic disease status materials of the Korea Disease Control and Prevention Agency (KDCA) (2023, 2025) present the population requiring management for metabolic diseases such as hypertension and diabetes, and for dementia, as large, alongside population aging. Meanwhile, the population of hepatitis B virus carriers is large worldwide, and it has been noted in a WHO document that a considerable share of patients with chronic hepatitis B and C are unaware of their infection (WHO EB134.R18, 2014). In addition, low medication adherence in long-term treatment has been discussed across chronic diseases in general (WHO, 2003; Khan & Socha-Dietrich, 2018). Accordingly, rather than making unwarranted claims of cure, the need to secure long-term continuity of management by maintaining continuous medication use and self-management after diagnosis so that values are "maintained within the target range" is being discussed in the health literature (see Chapter 6).

This framework defines as its top priority the sustainability of on-site management adherence and the prevention of dropout from management through relief of economic burden, and the securing of stable, continuous management, rather than short-term fluctuations in values or assertive claims of effect.

Inheriting the soma-moa "Surviving Together" philosophy and Charter 0 (Axiom 0: the Principle of Auxiliary Governance), the model integrates a first tier (Tier 1), in which social and institutional support systems interlock with the patient's continuous management efforts, and a second tier (Tier 2), in which positive psychological responses formed through objective confirmation of management results feed back into continued management. Furthermore, by excluding assertive statements of therapeutic effect and pursuing management grounded in the objective standards of the healthcare system, it aims at forming a sustainable public health infrastructure that goes beyond bearing burdens alone.

## Chapter 2: Core System Structure and Upper-Level Concepts

This system is defined as an upper-level structure in which the assumption of institutional support, continuous management adherence, objective indicator evaluation, and indirect psychological responses are organically linked.

### 1. Upper Structure (Multi-Tier Architecture)

- **Tier 1 (Institutional Support and Management Practice Layer)** — Assumes an arrangement in which the patient's out-of-pocket burden is eased to a manageable level on the premise of health insurance coverage, and within that support framework, regular monitoring and lifestyle management are carried out continuously.
- **Tier 2 (Indicator Evaluation and Psychological Feedback Layer)** — Objectively determines management results according to the standards of the healthcare system, and applies a cyclical structure in which the psychological stability formed on the basis of objectively confirmed management results supports continued management.
- **Human-Facing Extension (Human-Facing Adherence Support Layer)** — Adopting the soma-moa "Quiet Assist" philosophy, it includes an indirect adherence-support system that minimizes adherence fatigue caused by excessive control or surveillance and helps individual chronic disease patients, weary from long-term management, maintain self-efficacy and psychological acceptance.
- **Mapping of the four virtuous-cycle elements to the tiers** — Tier 1 consists of "1) institutional support (assumption)" and "2) steady management (practice)", while Tier 2 operates in correspondence with "3) maintenance within the target range (evaluation)" and "4) positive thinking (indirect response)".

### 2. Definition of Core Terms and Scope of Application

- **Maintenance within the target range** — As the core indicator of the proposed framework, it means a state in which the patient's biomarkers, viral suppression values (such as HBV DNA), cognitive function assessment scores (such as MMSE/CDR), and clinical indicators are stably regulated within a set management target range.
- **Upper-level inclusive definition of target disease groups** — As an upper-level concept, it encompasses metabolic chronic diseases such as hypertension and diabetes; chronic liver disease groups such as hepatitis B carriers, which require continuous antiviral medication and monitoring; and degenerative brain disease groups such as Alzheimer's disease and mild cognitive impairment, which require long-term medication adherence management.
- **Classification of indicator types** — The starting point (management after diagnosis is confirmed) and the outcome (continuation of long-term management) are common to all target disease groups, and the indicators observed in between are divided into three types. Type A (threshold-maintenance type) is an indicator such as blood pressure or blood glucose whose goal is to stay at or below a reference value; Type B (suppression-maintenance type) is an indicator such as HBV DNA that looks at staying below a detection limit; Type C (progress-tracking type) is an indicator such as cognitive function scores (MMSE, CDR, etc.) that tracks change relative to a baseline point rather than a fixed target value. The conditional formulas for each type are set out in Chapter 5.
- **Explicit boundary of scope** — The scope of this model is limited to the stage of long-term management and continued medication adherence after diagnosis is confirmed; the pre-diagnosis stage (accessibility of screening and early detection of disease) is defined as outside the direct scope of this model.
- **Explicit healthcare-system standard** — The determination of whether the "target range is maintained" does not depend on the patient's subjective perception or non-standard recognition; it follows the clinical standards of the standardized healthcare system and the objective judgment of the attending medical staff.
- **Objectivity of indicator setting** — Evaluation expressions that may be uncertain or exaggerated are excluded at the outset, and only clinically verifiable, stably regulated states are set as core management goals.

## Chapter 3: Detailed Virtuous-Cycle Model Structure

This virtuous-cycle model is set up as a structure in which four main elements act sequentially and in feedback.

### 1. Institutional Support (Assumed Premise)

- **Basic assumption** — Assumes a support arrangement that, on the premise of health insurance coverage and national health insurance benefit guidelines, lowers the patient's out-of-pocket burden to a manageable level.
- **Exclusion of assertions** — Detailed scope of coverage, support ratios, and policy decisions, such as reimbursement criteria for hepatitis B antivirals and the scope of Alzheimer's treatment and care support, are not asserted definitively and are specified as an area of assumption that may vary as the environment changes.

### 2. Steady Management (Behavioral Practice)

- **Content of practice** — A stage in which, within the stable framework provided by institutional support, the patient continuously performs medication adherence (adherence to prescribed hepatitis B antivirals, adherence to prescribed Alzheimer's medications, etc.), regular checkups, and self-management such as diet and exercise.
- **Mitigation of dropout** — In connection with the premise of eased economic burden, it aims to reduce the likelihood of giving up on long-term management and of medication discontinuation.

### 3. Maintenance within the Target Range (Outcome Evaluation)

- **Criterion of judgment** — The sole objective outcome criterion for evaluating whether steady management has been carried out.
- **Stable regulation** — Continuously verifies that blood glucose and blood pressure values, the state of suppression of hepatitis B virus replication, and Alzheimer's cognitive function indicators are maintained without deviating from the individual target range set by the healthcare system, and specifies that determination is based on the objective standards of the healthcare system. Depending on the indicator type, "maintenance within the target range" is judged as staying at or below a reference value (Type A), staying below a detection limit (Type B), or staying within an allowable change range relative to a baseline point (Type C).

### 4. Positive Thinking (Indirect Response)

- **Condition of occurrence** — Described as a psychological response that can be expected only when "maintenance within the target range" has been objectively confirmed through medical staff and test values.
- **Prohibition of direct-effect statements** — It is strictly prohibited to state that positive thinking or a psychological attitude has a direct therapeutic effect on biomarkers or on the disease itself.
- **Definition of the indirect pathway** — Positive responses are described only as a possible indirect pathway that relieves the patient's psychological stress and helps continue "steady management".

## Chapter 4: Common Layer — Indirect Pathways and Long-Term Observation Methodology

### 1. Structure of Psychological and Behavioral Indirect Pathways

- **Auxiliary factor** — Positive thinking is not a direct clinical treatment; it operates as an auxiliary factor that sustains the behavioral adherence rate.
- **Adherence feedback** — An indirect feedback loop is formed, running from [confirmation of maintenance within the target range] to [formation of a positive response] to [strengthening of motivation for steady management], thereby enhancing the stability of long-term management.

### 2. Study Design and Prevention of Selection Bias

- **Variability of prior study results** — Because existing self-management intervention studies conducted on patients with chronic disease (metabolic disease, hepatitis B carriers, Alzheimer's patients, etc.) show differences and variability depending on patient group and observation conditions, no particular tendency is asserted as a fixed fact.
- **Risk of selection bias** — If, in order to reduce analytic error or variability, the study population is arbitrarily narrowed or only highly adherent patients are selected, the risk increases that selection bias will arise, so that the sample is not representative of the chronic disease population as a whole.
- **Principle of cohort follow-up** — To secure methodological safety, the cohort sample is comprehensively defined at the start of the study or demonstration, and long-term follow-up without arbitrary exclusion during the course is set as the standard.
- **ITT-like principle and handling of missing data** — In the analysis of observational cohort studies, an intention-to-treat-like principle (ITT-like Principle) is applied so that dropouts and missing data are reflected conservatively rather than arbitrarily excluded, thereby mitigating selection bias.

## Chapter 5: Mathematical Model and Limitations

### 1. Model of Regulation within the Target Range (by Indicator Type)

The degree to which a patient's clinical indicators are maintained within the standards of the healthcare system is modeled by the following conditional formulas according to the indicator type. The three types share the starting point of long-term management after diagnosis is confirmed and the outcome of continued long-term management, and differ only in the nature of the indicator observed in between. The index is a summary indicator for progress observation and is not an indicator for determining treatment effect.

#### A. Types A and B (Threshold-Maintenance Type and Suppression-Maintenance Type)

```
S_index(t) = max(0, 1 - max(0, V_curr(t) - V_target) / V_margin)
```

- `S_index(t)` — Index of regulation within the target range at time t (`0 <= S_index(t) <= 1`)
- `V_curr(t)` — Actual clinical value measured at time t (blood glucose, blood pressure, HBV DNA level, etc.)
- `V_target` — Type A: upper-limit threshold of the management target under standard healthcare-system guidelines. Type B: detection-limit criterion (such as the criterion for undetectable HBV DNA)
- `V_margin` — Maximum deviation limit allowed by the healthcare system (if `V_curr(t) >= V_target + V_margin`, then `S_index(t) = 0`)
- Whether to end or discontinue treatment for Type B follows the judgment of the attending medical staff and clinical guidelines, not this index, and this model does not replace it.

#### B. Type C (Progress-Tracking Type)

```
D(t) = V_base - V_curr(t)  (for indicators where higher is better, e.g., MMSE)
D(t) = V_curr(t) - V_base  (for indicators where lower is better, e.g., CDR-SB)
S_index(t) = max(0, 1 - max(0, D(t)) / D_margin)
```

- `V_base` — Measured value at the baseline point of the start of management
- `D(t)` — Extent of deterioration relative to the baseline point (if there is no change or there is improvement, `max(0, D(t)) = 0` and `S_index(t) = 1`)
- `D_margin` — Maximum allowable change range permitted by the healthcare system (if `D(t) >= D_margin`, then `S_index(t) = 0`)
- For Type C, "maintenance within the target range" is read as "maintenance within an allowable change range relative to the baseline point", and `D_margin` may vary depending on the natural course of the disease and the individual's condition.

**Common:** The direction of each indicator (higher is better or lower is better) and the values of `V_target`, `V_margin`, and `D_margin` follow the standards of the healthcare system, and this white paper does not present specific numerical values (see Chapter 9). For bidirectional interval-target indicators (e.g., a specific permissible range of blood glucose or blood pressure), the absolute deviation `|V_curr(t) - V_target|` may be adapted and used.

### 2. Weight Model for Acceptance of Indirect Adherence Feedback

The weight `W_adherence` for indirect psychological responses and behavioral adherence that help continuous management is calculated by the following conditional formula.

```
W_adherence = β_base + α · S_bar_index
```

- `W_adherence` — Weight for maintaining the patient's continuous management adherence (`0 <= W_adherence <= 1`)
- `β_base` — Baseline management adherence secured by institutional support (relief of out-of-pocket burden) (`0 <= β_base <= 1`)
- `S_bar_index` — Average "maintenance within the target range" regulation index observed during a recent designated observation period (e.g., the most recent N months) (`0 <= S_bar_index <= 1`) (the `S_index` of all types is normalized to 0–1 and averaged in the same way.)
- `α` — Indirect influence coefficient by which confirmation of maintenance within the target range leads to psychological stability and steady management (`0 <= α <= 1 - β_base`; limited to an auxiliary coefficient for sustaining behavior, not a direct treatment coefficient)

### 3. Analytical Limitation of the Dynamic Feedback Structure

This model has a dynamic mutual-feedback (Feedback Loop) structure in which continuous management raises the maintenance index, and the objectively confirmed index in turn reinforces continuous management. Accordingly, it is specified that there is an endogeneity limitation: in empirical validation, it is difficult to separate and identify a purely one-directional causal relationship independently.

## Chapter 6: Prior-Art References and Distinctiveness

> The citations of prior art and research in this chapter are preliminary references and are not a precise literature cross-check.

### 1. Academic Literature and Health Trend References

- **References on trends of global organizations and public health agencies** — The WHO Executive Board resolution on hepatitis (2014) has noted the problem that many patients with chronic hepatitis B and C are unaware of their infection, and the WHO report on adherence to long-term therapies (2003) and the OECD Health Working Papers (Khan & Socha-Dietrich, 2018) have discussed the importance of improving medication adherence in terms of health outcomes and fiscal efficiency. In addition, KDCA's Chronic Disease Status and Issues (2023, 2025) presents management indicators such as awareness, treatment, and control rates for major chronic diseases including hypertension, diabetes, and hypercholesterolemia.
- **References on guidelines for chronic viral liver disease and hepatitis B management** — The Korean Association for the Study of the Liver (KASL, 2022) clinical practice guidelines for chronic hepatitis B have presented sustained suppression of HBV replication as the ultimate goal of treatment, have noted that the HBV DNA level is used as an indicator of disease progression and long-term prognosis in judging treatment endpoints, and have recommended regular follow-up even after treatment cessation. The WHO (2024) guidelines on chronic hepatitis B have addressed strategies to promote adherence to long-term antiviral therapy and retention in care as one of their main topics.
- **References on drug-treatment guidelines for Alzheimer's disease and cognitive disorders** — The Korean Dementia Association (KDA, 2025) clinical practice guidelines for dementia (recommendations on cholinesterase inhibitors and memantine) have recommended cholinesterase inhibitors in Alzheimer's disease for improving cognitive and daily function.
- **Prior studies on chronic disease self-management** — The importance of self-management education and support, and cases of integrating it into healthcare system improvement, have been discussed (Bodenheimer et al., 2002; Glasgow et al., 2002).
- **Studies on out-of-pocket relief and medication adherence** — An association between healthcare financial support or reduced out-of-pocket burden and patients' medication adherence and long-term management has been reported (Goldman et al., 2007; Choudhry et al., 2011).
- **Studies on psychological factors and behavioral practice** — It has been reported that emotional states such as depression are associated with treatment non-adherence (DiMatteo et al., 2000).
- **Studies on selection bias and methodology** — The types and structure of selection bias arising in study design and analysis, and methods for correcting it, have been discussed (Sackett, 1979; Hernán et al., 2004).
- **International and European guideline references** — The WHO guideline on the pharmacological treatment of hypertension in adults (2021) has addressed thresholds for starting treatment, follow-up intervals, and target blood pressure for control; the European Association for the Study of the Liver (EASL, 2025) hepatitis B guidelines have addressed treatment goals, treatment indications and cessation, and monitoring during treatment; and the European ABC Project (Vrijens et al., 2012) has presented a taxonomy that divides the process of medication adherence into stages such as initiation and implementation.
- **References on cognitive assessment tools** — The Mini-Mental State Examination (MMSE) has been proposed as a method of assessing cognitive status by score (Folstein et al., 1975), and KDCA's dementia status materials (2023) present a classification of dementia severity using the Clinical Dementia Rating (CDR).

### 2. No Efficacy Claims and Clarification of Distinctiveness

- **Statement of no efficacy claims** — This white paper does not claim the therapeutic efficacy of any particular practical method or therapy, any effect of curing disease, or any direct improvement in biomarker values. References to prior studies are invoked solely to provide defensive disclosure and conceptual background, and are not directly combined or linked with the effects of the individual model of this white paper.
- **Linkage to the soma-moa internal ecosystem** — The deterministic thresholds and sustained-feedback logic of this white paper state an internal-ecosystem cross-reference (PHILOSOPHY.ko.md) that extends, from a healthcare perspective, the "Surviving Together" philosophy of the soma-moa governance architecture and the state-transition logic of Charter 0 (Axiom 0: the Principle of Auxiliary Governance).
- **Clarification of distinctiveness** — While excluding assertive or uncertain expressions of outcome, it sets "maintenance within the target range" based on the healthcare-system standard as the core evaluation indicator, and at the same time describes psychological responses only as an indirect pathway that helps management rather than as a direct therapeutic effect, thereby securing the novelty of a defensive virtuous-cycle model free of exaggeration.

- **Statement of the pre-validation stage** — The virtuous-cycle model and mathematical formula system presented in this white paper are a descriptive hypothetical system for which clinical validation has not been completed, and the current stage is a conceptual stage in which precise exploratory clinical research and empirical validation must be carried out in the future.

- **Statement of distinction of application area** — The application area of this model is limited to self-management and medication adherence management after diagnosis is confirmed, and linkage with early screening systems at the pre-diagnosis stage is deferred to a separate future research task.
- **Statement on the need for further review** — With regard to the specific influence of individual self-management intervention elements on behavioral maintenance and indicator regulation, no assertive effect is claimed; it is described only at the level of a hypothesis that multifaceted further review and long-term cohort analysis are needed on the related possibilities.
